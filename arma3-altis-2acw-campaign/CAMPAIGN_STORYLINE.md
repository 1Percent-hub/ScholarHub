# Arma 3 Campaign: **Altis Crossfire**  
## A 2ACW Storyline on Altis

*The Second American Civil War didn’t stay in America. When the EU intervened and rival factions sent “advisors” and contractors abroad, the Mediterranean became a proxy battleground. Altis—strategic, neutral, and far from home—is where the 2ACW factions have come to fight their war by other means.*

---

## Lore (Read This If You Want the Full Story)

What follows is the worldbuilding and backstory behind the campaign. You can read it for atmosphere, brief your players, or ignore it and jump to factions and missions.

### How the War Started

The Second American Civil War (2ACW) didn’t begin on a single day. It was the result of years of political fracture: disputed elections, state secessions, and the collapse of trust between the federal government and large parts of the country. When President Dennison declared a “national restoration” and ordered the military to “pacify” regions that had voted for independence or autonomy, units split. Some stayed loyal to Washington (Federalists). Others sided with breakaway states or with the “legitimate” government-in-exile that the opposition had formed (the side that would later call itself the NPA and its allies). Alaska, California, Hawaii, and others declared they would not obey federal orders that targeted American citizens. Texas declared neutrality and started selling to everyone. Militias formed on both sides—Pro-Dennison loyalists in some states, anti-Federal “Minutemen” and regional groups like the Portland-Maoists and 7CHAT in others. The European Union, fearing spillover and refugee flows, intervened with a “humanitarian and stability” mission that quickly put it at odds with Federalist policy. The war went from protests and standoffs to open fighting. No one has won. No one has surrendered.

### Why It Spread Overseas

America’s collapse didn’t stay contained. Allies and enemies alike had interests: trade routes, bases, intelligence, and ideological stakes. The EU wanted to protect its interests and prevent the conflict from spreading into Europe. Breakaway states needed supply lines, safe havens, and legitimacy. The Federal government needed deniable ways to hit its enemies abroad. Neutral parties—Texas, Florida Alliance, various mercenary outfits—saw profit. So “advisors,” “contractors,” and “humanitarian” missions began to appear wherever the war could be fought by other means: training, arms deals, raids, and proxy battles. The Mediterranean became one of those arenas. It was close to Europe, full of shipping lanes, and had plenty of unstable or ungoverned spaces. Altis was one of them.

### Why Altis

Altis is an island in the Mediterranean. In this timeline it has no standing army of its own—no AAF, no NATO, no CSAT. It’s a strategic piece of real estate: a potential logistics hub, a place to train, resupply, and run operations without the scrutiny of the mainland. When the 2ACW went global, every side wanted a piece. The EU set up a “stability” presence. California and other breakaway states established aid posts and supply depots. The Federalists sent “training” detachments that were really combat units. National Guard and Police elements followed. Mercenaries showed up on contract. Independents—Texas, Florida Alliance, Minutemen, Sheriff-led districts, 7CHAT, Militia, Portland-Maoists, Puerto Rico—either took sides or stayed neutral and sold to the highest bidder. Within months, Altis was no longer neutral. It was a second front: smaller, messier, and just as deadly.

### The Situation When the Campaign Begins

By the time your story starts, the island is divided into zones of influence. No one controls everything. Federalists and National Guard hold key airfields and checkpoints. California runs a critical aid post near Kavala; the EU has a training base and intel hub. Hawaii and Alaska have small but important footprints. NPA forces coordinate with the “coalition” but answer to their own chain of command. Mercenaries and Pro-Dennison Militia carry out raids and reprisals. Independents control villages, valleys, and smuggling routes. Colonel Richard “Dick” Harlow, the Federalist commander on Altis, is building something big in the north—a staging base for men and matériel. The coalition doesn’t yet know the full picture. You’re a contractor who got cut off when the fighting spread. You’re not here to save the world. You’re here to survive, protect your people, and maybe—if you choose—help decide who wins this corner of the war.

## Setting

**Location:** Altis (present day)  
**Context:** 2ACW-aligned forces have moved in: some officially “humanitarian” or “training,” others deniable. The island is a proxy battleground with no native military—only 2ACW factions fighting for control. The player’s story is caught in the crossfire.

**Tone:** Gritty, morally grey, no clear “good guy”—survival and choosing the least bad option.

## Main Characters

### **Player character (you)**

- **Name:** Your choice (suggested: **Marcus Webb**).
- **Background:** Former US Army, separated before the 2ACW. You came to Altis as a contractor, got cut off when the fighting spread, and now work for whoever keeps you and your small team alive—often the EU or California forces, but not exclusively.
- **Trait:** No grand loyalty; you care about your people and the civilians caught in the crossfire.

---

### **BLUFOR / Allies**

1. **Captain Elena Vasquez** – (2ACW) California  
   - Runs the California Republic aid post near Kavala. Medic by training, de facto leader.  
   - **Role:** Mission giver (humanitarian convoys, rescues, escort).  
   - **Arc:** Learns that one of her convoys was hit by Mercenaries on Federalist payroll; she wants justice, then revenge. You can temper or enable that.

2. **Sergeant Dmitri “Dima” Volkov** – (2ACW) European Union  
   - EU “training” detachment; ex–Eastern Bloc, speaks Russian and Greek.  
   - **Role:** Intel and joint ops (recon, raids on Federalist depots).  
   - **Arc:** Discovers Federalists are receiving outside arms and building a major staging base; conflict between EU rules of engagement and what he knows must be done.

3. **Lieutenant James “Jimmy” Okuda** – (2ACW) Hawaii  
   - Small Hawaiian recon team; drone and radio expert.  
   - **Role:** Recon missions, hacking, “find the high-value target.”  
   - **Arc:** Tracks a Federalist commander who turns out to be his former CO; moral choice between duty and past loyalty.

4. **Corporal Rosa Méndez** – (2ACW) NPA  
   - NPA liaison to “coalition” forces; by-the-book but pragmatic.  
   - **Role:** Official “loyalist” missions (capture HVTs, secure documents).  
   - **Arc:** Has to decide whether to report Vasquez’s revenge op or look the other way.

5. **Major Tom Bradley** – (2ACW) Alaska  
   - Alaska contingent CO; cold, efficient, focused on supply routes.  
   - **Role:** Logistics and “secure the LZ” / “hold the FOB” missions.  
   - **Arc:** His need for fuel and ammo pushes him to cut a deal with Texas; you can support or expose it.

---

### **OPFOR / Antagonists**

6. **Colonel Richard “Dick” Harlow** – (2ACW) Federalists  
   - Federalist commander on Altis; ex–Special Forces, runs deniable ops.  
   - **Role:** Recurring villain; appears in intel, then in person in the finale.  
   - **Arc:** You learn he’s not just a zealot—he’s building a Federalist staging base on Altis for a push back home. Stopping him becomes personal after he orders a hit on Vasquez’s clinic.

7. **“Judge” Morrison** – (2ACW) Pro-Dennison Militia  
   - Fanatical militia leader; runs “people’s courts” and reprisals in villages.  
   - **Role:** Side villain; terror missions, hostage situations.  
   - **Arc:** Optional mission to capture or eliminate him; affects Minutemen and Sheriff faction standing.

8. **Unnamed Mercenary Commander** – (2ACW) Mercenaries  
   - Voice on the radio, never fully seen; executes Federalist contracts.  
   - **Role:** Ambushes, convoy hits, assassination attempts on Vasquez and the player.  
   - **Arc:** Jimmy traces payments to a front company; you raid the Mercenary HQ in a night op.

---

### **Independents**

9. **“Doc” Rivera** – (2ACW) Puerto Rico  
   - Runs the Puerto Rico medical station on the south coast.  
   - **Role:** Healing, intel on local movement, safe house.  
   - **Arc:** Asks you to escort a wounded Federalist defector; choice to hand him to NPA, EU, or let him disappear.

10. **Carson Reed** – (2ACW) Texas  
    - Texas “liaison”; arms dealer with a cowboy hat and a spreadsheet.  
    - **Role:** Black-market missions: “acquire” crates, “lose” a convoy so Texas can resell.  
    - **Arc:** You can work with him and earn gear, or turn his deals over to Vasquez/Bradley and lock him out.

11. **Maria “Mama” Kostas** – (2ACW) Sheriff  
    - Local figure; one of the few “Sheriff” faction leaders with a name. Holds a district together with a mix of law and grit.  
    - **Role:** Village defence, “protect the harvest,” deal with Militia or bandits.  
    - **Arc:** Her son is conscripted by Pro-Dennison; you can rescue him and earn permanent Sheriff support.

12. **Viktor** – (2ACW) 7CHAT  
    - 7CHAT community leader; practical, suspicious of outsiders.  
    - **Role:** “Bring us medicine/ammo/food” in exchange for intel and guides.  
    - **Arc:** Trust builds over missions; eventually he reveals a hidden Federalist cache—optional big score or hand-over to EU.

13. **“Red”** – (2ACW) Portland-Maoists  
    - Ideological but pragmatic; cares more about hurting Federalists than theory.  
    - **Role:** Sabotage and propaganda missions; optional ally for one-off strikes.  
    - **Arc:** Asks you to hit a Federalist radio tower; if you do it, NPA and EU reputation drops; if you refuse, you lose a potential distraction for the finale.

14. **Old Man Hayes** – (2ACW) Minutemen  
    - Elder Minuteman; holds a valley with a few farmers and hunters.  
    - **Role:** Defend the valley from Militia or Federalist raids; he provides scouts and early warning later.  
    - **Arc:** If you save his granddaughter from a Pro-Dennison raid, Minutemen become a permanent recon asset.

15. **“Ghost”** – (2ACW) Florida Alliance  
    - Smuggler and fixer; no real name.  
    - **Role:** Get someone in or out, move cargo, “no questions.”  
    - **Arc:** Offers to smuggle Harlow off the island for a price; you can take the deal (Harlow lives, you get paid), refuse (moral win), or set a trap and hand Harlow to the EU.

## Campaign Structure (Mission Arc)

### **Act 1: Survivors (Missions 1–3)**  
*Theme: Find your place.*

- **1. “Leftovers”** – You and a few other contractors are cut off near Kavala. Federalists and Militia are clashing; you link up with California (Vasquez) and secure the aid post.  
  *Factions: California, Militia, Federalists.*

- **2. “First Contact”** – EU (Dima) needs a recon of a Federalist checkpoint. You go in with Hawaiian support (Jimmy); first mention of “Harlow” and Mercenary activity.  
  *Factions: EU, Hawaii, Federalists, National Guard.*

- **3. “The Doctor’s Price”** – Doc Rivera’s station is hit by Pro-Dennison. You defend it, then escort a convoy to 7CHAT; Viktor doesn’t trust you yet.  
  *Factions: Puerto Rico, Pro-Dennison Militia, 7CHAT.*

### **Act 2: Alliances (Missions 4–7)**  
*Theme: Who do you trust?*

- **4. “Texas Tea”** – Bradley (Alaska) needs fuel. Texas (Reed) has it—for a “favour”: redirect a Federalist convoy. Moral choice: do the deal or report it.  
  *Factions: Alaska, Texas, Federalists, National Guard.*

- **5. “Mama’s Boy”** – Sheriff (Maria Kostas) asks you to get her son back from Pro-Dennison. Infiltration or assault; “Judge” Morrison appears.  
  *Factions: Sheriff, Pro-Dennison Militia, optional Minutemen.*

- **6. “Red Lines”** – Red (Portland-Maoists) offers to hit a Federalist broadcast tower to distract them. Accept or refuse; NPA (Méndez) warns you not to work with “extremists.”  
  *Factions: Portland-Maoists, NPA, Federalists, Police.*

- **7. “Jimmy’s Ghost”** – Jimmy traces the Mercenary paymaster; you raid the Mercenary FOB at night. You capture intel pointing to Harlow and a major Federalist operation.  
  *Factions: Hawaii, Mercenaries, Federalists.*

### **Act 3: Crossfire (Missions 8–10)**  
*Theme: The war comes to a head.*

- **8. “Vasquez’s Revenge”** – Vasquez wants to hit the Federalist cell that ordered the strike on her clinic. Dima and Méndez are split. You lead the raid; Harlow escapes but you take a key lieutenant.  
  *Factions: California, EU, NPA, Federalists, National Guard.*

- **9. “The Valley”** – Old Man Hayes (Minutemen) calls: Federalists and Mercenaries are advancing. You defend the valley with Minutemen and whoever you’ve allied with (Sheriff, 7CHAT, etc.).  
  *Factions: Minutemen, Sheriff, 7CHAT, Federalists, Mercenaries.*

- **10. “Altis Crossfire” (Finale)** – Intel says Harlow is at his FOB (north Altis). Coalition (EU, California, Hawaii, NPA, Alaska) assaults; you lead the breach.  
  - **Optional:** Ghost offers to smuggle Harlow out; you can intercept and hand him to EU, or let him go for a payoff.  
  - **Climax:** Confrontation with Harlow—capture (EU/NPA want him alive) or kill.  
  *Factions: All BLUFOR, Federalists, National Guard, Mercenaries, optional Texas/Florida.*

## How Missions Should Go (Design Guide)

This section is for mission authors and Zeus GMs: how each mission could flow, what the player should feel, and what success/failure look like. Adapt to your playstyle (Zeus, scripted, co-op, etc.).

### General Principles

- **Briefing:** Short and clear. Who’s giving the mission, what they want, and what’s at stake. Optionally mention faction standing (e.g. “Vasquez won’t work with us again if we blow this”).
- **Insertion:** Start the player with a clear insertion (vehicle drop, march from FOB, boat). Keep travel time tight unless the mission is deliberately about a long approach.
- **Objectives:** One to three primary objectives per mission. Optionally one or two secondaries (e.g. rescue a specific NPC, grab intel, don’t destroy a building). Mark them so the player always knows the next step.
- **Choices:** Where the story allows, give a real choice (e.g. hand the defector to NPA or to EU; do the Texas deal or report it). The outcome doesn’t have to be “good vs bad”—often it’s “which ally do we please.”
- **Pacing:** Mix action (contact, assault, defence) with short breathers (moving between objectives, brief dialogue). Avoid long empty stretches.
- **Exfil / End:** Clear end condition (all objectives done + reach exfil / return to FOB / hold until timer). Optionally a short debrief line from the mission giver.

---

### Mission 1: “Leftovers”

**Feel:** Disoriented, then focused. You’re cut off; the goal is to find friends and secure a foothold.

- **Start:** Player + a few AI (or co-op buddies) near Kavala. Radio is chaos: Federalist and Militia traffic. No clear chain of command.
- **Primary 1:** Reach the California aid post (Vasquez) — follow marker or intel (e.g. “head for the old factory east of Kavala”).
- **Primary 2:** Defend the aid post from one or two waves (Federalists and/or Militia pushing in). Hold until Vasquez says “we’re good” or a timer ends.
- **Secondary (optional):** Evacuate wounded to the aid post before the waves hit (adds a time/route pressure).
- **End:** Vasquez thanks you and folds you into her roster. Brief dialogue: “You’re with us now. We need people who can fight and think.”
- **Fail:** Aid post is overrun or Vasquez is killed (if you use that). Player can respawn at last checkpoint or mission fails.

---

### Mission 2: “First Contact”

**Feel:** Recon and restraint. You’re working with new allies (EU, Hawaii); first hint of a bigger enemy (Harlow, Mercenaries).

- **Start:** Briefing from Dima (EU). Objective: recon a Federalist checkpoint, identify strength and any “irregular” (Mercenary) presence. Jimmy (Hawaii) on radio for intel/drone support if you want to script it.
- **Insertion:** Drop or march to a rally point; then approach the checkpoint on foot (or one vehicle, then dismount).
- **Primary 1:** Reach observation position and “mark” the checkpoint (use binoculars / designated action / waypoint).
- **Primary 2:** Identify and report “Harlow” (name on radio or on a document) and/or Mercenary presence (e.g. non-standard vehicles or uniforms). Can be a simple “confirm intel” objective.
- **Secondary (optional):** Don’t engage—recon only. If the player triggers a firefight, Dima is unhappy but mission can still succeed.
- **End:** Exfil to EU rally point. Dima: “Good work. That name—Harlow—we’ll dig. Stay sharp.”
- **Fail:** Player is wiped before reporting; or (if you use it) compromise the op by going loud too early and losing the intel.

---

### Mission 3: “The Doctor’s Price”

**Feel:** Defend then escort. Protect someone who helps everyone; earn 7CHAT’s distrust then a sliver of trust.

- **Start:** Doc Rivera’s station is under attack (Pro-Dennison). Player gets a call or briefing: defend the station, then escort a convoy to 7CHAT.
- **Primary 1:** Defend the medical station for X minutes or until Pro-Dennison retreat. Use a timer and 1–2 assault waves.
- **Primary 2:** Escort convoy (one or two vehicles + Rivera or a rep) to 7CHAT location. Ambush possible en route (Pro-Dennison or Militia).
- **Primary 3:** Hand over supplies at 7CHAT; Viktor is cold but accepts. Short dialogue: “We’ll see if you’re useful.”
- **Secondary (optional):** Keep all convoy vehicles and the principal alive.
- **End:** Return to California aid post or Rivera’s station. Rivera thanks you; Viktor’s standing goes from “hostile” to “neutral” or “cautious.”
- **Fail:** Station overrun, or convoy destroyed / principal killed.

---

### Mission 4: “Texas Tea”

**Feel:** Moral grey. You’re doing a favour for Texas so Alaska gets fuel; the “favour” is shady.

- **Start:** Bradley (Alaska) needs fuel. Reed (Texas) has it—in exchange for redirecting or “losing” a Federalist convoy so Texas can “acquire” it. Player is the one who does the redirect (ambush, reroute, or intel leak).
- **Primary 1:** Intercept or redirect the Federalist convoy (e.g. ambush at a chokepoint, or guide them into an area where Texas “takes” the cargo). Design so the player has a clear action (attack, block road, mark for Texas).
- **Primary 2:** Secure or confirm the fuel for Bradley (e.g. meet Reed at a handoff, or deliver a token to Bradley).
- **Choice:** After the mission, player can report the deal to Vasquez or Dima (Bradley and Texas reputation drop; “loyal” outcome) or stay quiet (Texas and Alaska reputation up; “pragmatic” outcome). Can be a dialogue at debrief.
- **End:** Bradley gets his fuel. Either a clean debrief or a tense one if the player reported.
- **Fail:** Federalists destroy the convoy or wipe the player; fuel never reaches Bradley.

---

### Mission 5: “Mama’s Boy”

**Feel:** Personal stakes. Rescue mission with a face (Maria’s son); Judge Morrison as antagonist.

- **Start:** Maria “Mama” Kostas (Sheriff) asks you to get her son back from Pro-Dennison. Intel: he’s held at a compound or village.
- **Insertion:** Player (and optional fireteam) approach by vehicle or foot. Option for stealth approach or direct assault.
- **Primary 1:** Locate the son (marked NPC or building).
- **Primary 2:** Extract him (bring him to exfil or to Maria). Pro-Dennison will react; Judge Morrison can appear in person or on radio (“You’re not taking him.”).
- **Secondary (optional):** Capture or eliminate Judge Morrison (unlocks better Sheriff/Minutemen standing; if you use standing).
- **End:** Hand the son to Maria. She’s grateful; Sheriff (and optionally Minutemen) become solid allies. If Morrison was taken out, add a line about “one less butcher.”
- **Fail:** Son or Maria killed; or mission timer runs out (e.g. “they’re moving him at dawn”).

---

### Mission 6: “Red Lines”

**Feel:** Ideological friction. Working with Red (Portland-Maoists) helps the op but costs reputation with NPA/EU.

- **Start:** Red offers to hit a Federalist broadcast tower to create a distraction. Méndez (NPA) warns you not to work with “extremists.” Player chooses: accept Red’s help or refuse.
- **If accept:** Primary 1: Meet Red’s team (or do it yourself with Red’s intel). Primary 2: Destroy or disable the tower (explosives, breach, or sabotage). Federalists and Police respond. Primary 3: Exfil. NPA/EU standing drops; Red and Portland-Maoists become available for one-off help later (e.g. distraction in finale).
- **If refuse:** Alternate objective: e.g. a different distraction (Dima or Jimmy provides a recon or jamming task instead). No reputation hit; no Red asset for later.
- **End:** Debrief reflects the choice. Red thanks you or stays cold; Méndez either “understands” or “notes this for the record.”
- **Fail:** Tower mission: player wiped or tower not destroyed in time. Refuse path: fail only if the alternate objective fails.

---

### Mission 7: “Jimmy’s Ghost”

**Feel:** Night op, high stakes. Raid the Mercenary FOB; get intel that ties everything to Harlow.

- **Start:** Jimmy has traced the Mercenary paymaster to a FOB. Night insertion. Objective: raid the FOB, grab intel, get out.
- **Insertion:** Boat or foot at night. Optional: suppress or avoid patrols.
- **Primary 1:** Enter the FOB (breach a gate, clear a section, or stealth to the objective).
- **Primary 2:** Secure intel (laptop, documents, or designated object). Script a short “find” moment so it feels like a discovery.
- **Primary 3:** Exfil under pressure (Mercenaries and possibly Federalists reinforce). Timer or wave-based.
- **End:** Jimmy or Dima on radio: “This points to Harlow. He’s building something big. We need to hit him before it’s ready.”
- **Fail:** Player wiped; or intel destroyed (e.g. if you script a “destroy documents” trigger for the enemy).

---

### Mission 8: “Vasquez’s Revenge”

**Feel:** Coalition op with emotional drive. Vasquez wants the cell that hit her clinic; Harlow escapes but you take a lieutenant.

- **Start:** Joint op: California, EU, NPA elements. Vasquez leads the charge emotionally; Dima and Méndez are in support (or at odds over rules). Target: Federalist cell that ordered the clinic strike.
- **Insertion:** Combined force to the objective (compound or town). Can be a vehicle assault or dismounted approach.
- **Primary 1:** Breach and clear the objective. Resistance: National Guard + Federalists.
- **Primary 2:** Capture or eliminate the cell leader (lieutenant-level). Harlow should be mentioned on radio or in intel but not present—he “pulls out before you get there.”
- **Primary 3:** Exfil with prisoners or confirmation of the lieutenant’s fate.
- **End:** Vasquez: “It’s not Harlow, but it’s something.” Dima/Méndez: intel points to Harlow’s FOB in the north. Set up the finale.
- **Fail:** Coalition wiped; or lieutenant escapes (optional—you can allow “partial success” if only the lieutenant gets away).

---

### Mission 9: “The Valley”

**Feel:** Defence and alliance. Hold the line with Minutemen (and optionally Sheriff, 7CHAT) against a serious push.

- **Start:** Old Man Hayes (Minutemen) calls: Federalists and Mercenaries are advancing on the valley. Player must defend a position (village, FOB, or chokepoint) with Minutemen and any allies earned earlier.
- **Setup:** Player and friendlies at the defence point. Optional: place fortifications or call in support (if standing is high with EU/California).
- **Primary 1:** Hold for X waves or Y minutes. Each wave: Federalists and/or Mercenaries from one or two directions. Vary intensity (light vehicles, infantry, one heavy push).
- **Primary 2:** Keep Hayes (and optionally a key building or asset) alive. If he dies, mission can fail or become a “fighting retreat” alternate end.
- **End:** Enemy breaks off. Hayes: “We held. You’re one of us now.” Unlock or reinforce Minutemen/Sheriff/7CHAT support for the finale.
- **Fail:** Position overrun; Hayes or critical asset destroyed.

---

### Mission 10: “Altis Crossfire” (Finale)

**Feel:** Climactic assault; then a personal choice about Harlow.

- **Start:** Coalition (EU, California, Hawaii, NPA, Alaska) assaults Harlow’s FOB in the north. Player leads the breach or a key element. Optional: Ghost contacts you—he can smuggle Harlow out for a price. Player can agree, refuse, or set a trap.
- **Phase 1 – Assault:** Primary 1: Breach the FOB (gates, outer defences). Primary 2: Clear sectors and push to the command area. Heavy resistance: Federalists, National Guard, Mercenaries. Use vehicles and infantry; consider a timer (e.g. “Harlow’s evac in 15 minutes”).
- **Phase 2 – Harlow:** Primary 3: Confront Harlow. He can be in a bunker, a building, or at an evac point. Options: capture (EU/NPA want him alive), kill, or (if Ghost is in play) let him go in exchange for payoff / trap him and hand to EU.
- **Choice:** Capture = “justice” ending, intel from Harlow. Kill = “no loose ends.” Ghost deal = morally grey; trap = Ghost betrayed, Harlow to EU. Reflect in debrief.
- **End:** Short epilogue. Coalition holds the FOB. Vasquez, Dima, Jimmy, Méndez, Bradley get one line each. Player’s choices (Texas, Red, Sheriff, etc.) can be acknowledged in a final line or two.
- **Fail:** Coalition wiped; or Harlow escapes without being captured or killed (if you don’t want that as an intentional “bad” ending).

### Optional: Faction Standing and Mission Variants

If you use the optional Faction Standing system from the doc, consider:

- **Low California:** Mission 1 harder (less support at the aid post); Mission 8 Vasquez may not participate or may be wounded earlier.
- **Low EU:** Mission 2 and 7 less intel support; Mission 10 less EU forces; Dima may not offer certain dialogue.
- **Low Texas:** Mission 4 no deal (Bradley finds another way or mission changes); no black-market options later.
- **Low Sheriff / 7CHAT / Minutemen:** Mission 9 fewer friendlies or no early warning; Mission 5 may not be offered or Mama may be hostile.
- **Low NPA:** Mission 8 Méndez may report Vasquez; Mission 10 NPA may not commit or may demand Harlow alive with consequences if you kill him.

You can implement these as alternate briefings, different unit counts, or dialogue branches.

## Faction Standing (Optional System)

Track reputation with key groups; it can unlock missions or change dialogue:

- **California / Vasquez:** High = extra medical and humanitarian missions; low = no access to aid post.
- **EU / Dima:** High = better intel and joint ops; low = no EU support in finale.
- **Texas / Reed:** High = black market and “favours”; low = no deal in Mission 4, different Act 3 options.
- **Sheriff / 7CHAT / Minutemen:** High = scouts, safe passage, resupply; low = hostility or no help in Mission 9.
- **NPA / Méndez:** High = official “loyalist” support and intel; low = NPA may refuse to join finale or arrest Vasquez.

- **Setting:** Altis as a proxy battleground for 2ACW factions only (no AAF/NATO/CSAT).  
- **Player:** A cut-off contractor (e.g. Marcus Webb) who works with multiple factions.  
- **Characters:** 15 named characters across BLUFOR, OPFOR, and Independent 2ACW factions.  
- **Story:** Survive → build alliances → uncover Federalist staging plan → defend the island and decide Harlow’s fate.  
- **Missions:** 10 missions, with moral choices and faction standing affecting support and ending.  
- **How missions should go:** For each mission: suggested flow (briefing, insertion, objectives, choices, exfil), primary/secondary objectives, pacing, fail conditions, and optional faction-standing variants. Use it for Zeus, Eden, or scripted campaign design.

You can read the doc for the storyline and lore, or use the mission-design section to build or run the missions in Arma 3.
